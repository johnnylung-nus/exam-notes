Heuristics（啟發式；引導搜尋的估計） and admissibility（可採納性；不高估成本）

A heuristic estimates remaining cost, helping search focus on promising states. Admissible means it never overestimates the true cheapest remaining cost.

What this tells us: A single overestimate is enough to refute admissibility. Straight-line kilometres are a defensible road-distance bound, but cannot automatically estimate fares: a long subsidised trip can cost less than a short expensive one.

What makes a heuristic safe for optimal（最優） A* search（A星搜尋）?

  • A heuristic is not a promise that the next move is good. It supplies an estimate of the best remaining cost from a state. To justify（給出理由；用情境與證據支持選擇） admissibility, show why the estimate can never exceed a real feasible（可行；符合所有硬性條件） route under the chosen cost definition; a few accurate examples are not a proof.
  • More informative lower bounds can prune exploration（探索新選擇） by giving larger estimates that remain safe. Zero is always a safe bound for nonnegative remaining costs, but contributes no directional information. Two bounds cannot be compared only by their numeric（數值型；以數量表示） size if they estimate different units or use incompatible assumptions（假設；答案依賴但題目未明說的條件）.
  • Consistency（一致性） is a relationship between neighbouring estimates: the estimate before a move should not exceed that move’s cost plus the estimate afterwards. It supports settled-state graph-search behaviour. Admissibility alone concerns the cost to a goal; correct reopening can be needed when consistency is absent.
Implementation steps and their purpose
  1. Identify the actual objective and its units.
  2. Explain how the estimate relates to that objective.
  3. Check that the goal estimate is zero.
  4. Justify a lower bound, rather than checking only convenient examples.
  5. Use consistency or reopen improved states in graph search.

Use the result and check it

Data, components and application

  • Wrong units → align estimate with cost → kilometres cannot directly estimate fare.
  • Overestimation → revise lower bound → A* may otherwise choose a suboptimal goal.

How to evaluate it

Check estimates against known remaining costs; compare nodes expanded. Zero is admissible but gives no directional guidance and reduces A* to uniform-cost search（一致成本搜尋；先選累積成本最低者）.

Build the answer in this order

  1. State what cost is being estimated.
  2. Check lower-bound argument.
  3. Discuss informativeness.
  4. Connect conditions to algorithm guarantee.

Common wrong answers and the exact correction
  • A smaller estimate is not automatically more informative.
  • Admissible does not mean（均值；平均數） exact.

When would this calculation be useful?

Needed when choosing or checking an A* estimate; a plausible-looking estimate is not enough to claim optimality.
