Semantic Networks（語義網路）

Knowledge is represented by nodes and labelled relationships. The lecture’s two reasoning concepts are Inheritance（繼承） and Graph Traversal（沿圖走訪）. The labels in the diagram are examples used to apply them.

Lecture graphicInheritanceGraph traversalDifference / exam use

1. Inheritance（繼承）— use knowledge from a broader group

An individual or a more specific group can obtain applicable category information and general properties from the group it belongs to. You do not need to write the same shared information separately for every individual.

Mary → Female Persons → Persons → Mammals
  • Category information: Mary is a female person, so she is also a person and a mammal.
  • Shared property: the graph gives Persons a Legs value of 2. This is the general value available through the group.
  • Specific exception: John has his own explicit Legs value of 1. Keep that value; do not overwrite it with the general value 2.

Exam use: find the broader group’s relevant property, then check whether a more specific value or exception is given. Do not transfer properties through every kind of relationship: being someone’s sister does not mean copying all their properties.

2. Graph Traversal（沿圖走訪）— follow links to find information

Traversal means starting at a node and moving along the links needed to answer a question. Read each link’s meaning and direction as you go.

QuestionLinks to follow in the lecture graphResult
Is Mary a mammal?Mary → MemberOf → Female Persons → SubsetOf → Persons → SubsetOf → MammalsYes; the path establishes the group membership.
Who is Mary the sister of?Mary → SisterOf → JohnJohn.
What Legs value should be used for John?Start at John and check his directly stated Legs link.1; the specific value is already present.

The links tell you where to look; their meanings tell you what conclusion is justified. Do not reverse a one-way relationship or invent a missing link.

Keep the two concepts separate

ConceptMain questionIn this graph
Graph traversalWhich links do I follow to find the information?Follow Mary’s membership links until reaching Mammals.
InheritanceWhat category knowledge/property can this individual obtain from its group?Use the Persons property, but keep John’s explicitly stated exception.

If asked to draw: put the relevant entities/groups in nodes, label the relationships, then show the path or inherited property needed for the question. Keep MemberOf, SubsetOf and the other labels as the diagram’s relationships—not separate long lessons.

The lecture’s use cases: meaning and scenario clues

The use-case names come from the lecture. The short scenarios below explain how to recognize them in an exam; they are teaching examples, not quoted exam questions.

Lecture use caseWhat it meansExample / clue in a question
NLP — Natural Language Processing（自然語言處理）Use relationships between concepts to help understand or answer about language.“Is Mary a mammal?” → identify Mary in the network, follow the membership path, and answer from the connected knowledge.
Associative memory（關聯記憶）Recall related information when one item is given.Starting with Mary, follow SisterOf to retrieve John. This association retrieves a related person; it does not copy all of John’s properties to Mary.

Choose by the required job: explain what knowledge is stored and how this representation helps with the scenario. A use-case name alone is not a sufficient reason.
