EXTRA-KNOWLEDGE-ACQUISITION

Knowledge acquisition（知識擷取）: elicitation（向專家擷取知識） versus discovery

Elicitation obtains knowledge from specialists; discovery learns patterns from recorded cases. Both concern how the system’s knowledge is built, rather than where the finished system runs.

Answer the actual eye-diagnosis acquisition comparison

What this tells us: The comparison is about the source and reliability of knowledge. It does not compare retrieval（檢索） speed, cloud hosting, or whether the doctor clicks a button.

Should knowledge be elicited from experts or discovered from data?

  • In elicitation, the information source is the specialist’s experience. Interviewing and checking cases exposes distinctions that have not already been captured in a large training（訓練） table. The output could be rules, frames（框架；附欄位的知識記錄） or another explicit representation（表徵；資料的表示方式）; rules are not compulsory.
  • In discovery, the information source is historical evidence. The algorithm finds relationships between recorded observations（觀測值） and outcomes. Its conclusions depend on which cases were collected, how inputs and labels were recorded, and whether the training setup matches the future task.
  • The two routes can complement each other: mined patterns are candidates for specialist review, while expert knowledge can identify missing variables（變數） or implausible relationships. A pattern being frequent does not make it causally or clinically valid.
  • Acquisition happens when building or updating the knowledge. Runtime reasoning（推理） applies that knowledge to a current patient. A rule extracted yesterday and applied today is not an example of the deployed system learning during this consultation.

Common wrong answers and the exact correction
  • Experts do not guarantee correct or complete rules.
  • Discovery requires preparation and validation; it is not automatically faster or more accurate.
  • Do not replace the requested pros/cons comparison with “offline versus online” or “local versus cloud”.

Apply the distinction

Data, components and application

  • Specialist time is limited → prioritize high-impact distinctions and record the rationale → reduces repeated interviews and makes checking possible.
  • Rare conditions are poorly represented → obtain suitable examples or define an explicit limitation/fallback（備援處理） → a model cannot learn reliable rare-case patterns from absent evidence.
  • Incorrect labels or future information → verify diagnoses and keep only inputs available at the decision time → avoids learning misleading relationships.

How to evaluate it

Check whether the acquired knowledge improves diagnosis and referral decisions on held-out（留出資料；未參與該階段訓練） cases, including rare conditions and important error types. Specialist validation checks meaning; held-out evaluation（評估） checks generalization（泛化能力）. Neither alone proves perfect clinical performance.

Build the answer in this order

  1. Name both acquisition routes.
  2. Give one case-specific advantage and limitation for each.
  3. Tie claims to specialist expertise or patient-record quality.
  4. Keep runtime hosting and inference（推論；由依據得出結論） speed out of this acquisition comparison.

An answer you could write

  • Elicitation can obtain explicit eye-diagnosis/referral knowledge directly from specialists without requiring a large labelled patient dataset, but extracting and checking that expertise takes specialist time.
  • Discovery can learn symptom/sign–diagnosis associations from historical cases, but rare eye conditions and unreliable records limit what it can learn.
  • I would validate either source of knowledge against held-out cases and specialist judgement; the acquisition method alone does not guarantee diagnostic accuracy（準確率）.

Where this is tested in the sample papers

ISY5001 Q2(f) — Knowledge acquisition: elicitation versus discovery

What the question requires

  1. Compare how knowledge is acquired, not how it is deployed.
  2. Give one application-specific advantage and limitation for elicitation.
  3. Give one advantage and limitation for discovery.

Proposed answer points

  • Elicitation: specialists supply inspectable diagnosis/referral knowledge; obtaining and validating it takes scarce specialist time and tacit judgments may be hard to articulate.
  • Discovery: patient records can reveal symptom/sign associations; unreliable labels or too few rare-condition cases limit learning.

Avoid: Fast retrieval, offline use or centralised hosting do not distinguish elicitation from discovery.
