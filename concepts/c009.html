Deductive reasoning（演繹推理）

Deduction（演繹） applies a general rule to facts to obtain a conclusion（結論）. If the rule and premises（前提；推理的依據） are true and inference（推論；由依據得出結論） is valid, the conclusion follows logically.

Recognise this in a question: IF–THEN, expert rules, known facts, justify a decision

What this tells us: The second row is a hypothesis（假設；待驗證的解釋） problem, while the fourth exposes another invalid reversal. A condition not triggering this rule says nothing about other possible causes or rules.

When does a conclusion follow from given rules?

  • Deduction preserves truth from premises to conclusion when the inference is valid. The important exam condition is not simply that the rule exists: all required conditions must be established for the current case. An unknown symptom cannot be silently counted as absent or present to complete a rule.
  • A domain rule and an inference procedure have different roles. “If these findings then this conclusion” belongs in the KB; matching facts to that rule and adding its conclusion is what the engine does. Frames（框架；附欄位的知識記錄） can organise the facts without replacing this matching procedure.
  • An explainable answer names which observations（觀測值） and rules support the conclusion. It must also preserve the rule’s scope: a toy implication in a lecture is not a validated universal clinical statement. Logical correctness cannot establish the truth or completeness（完備性） of the expert knowledge supplied.
Implementation steps and their purpose
  1. Write the relevant rule clearly.
  2. Match its conditions against present facts.
  3. Check every required condition, including AND/OR and thresholds.
  4. Apply the conclusion and record the rule used.
  5. Continue if the new conclusion triggers another rule.

Prepare the data and validate the representation

Data, components and application

  • Unknown condition → ask or defer → missing is not automatically false.
  • Incorrect domain rule → expert validation and versioning → valid logic cannot rescue false premises.

How to evaluate it

Test ordinary cases, boundaries, missing facts and contradictory rules. Explain which facts and rules support the result.

Build the answer in this order

  1. State facts.
  2. Select applicable rule.
  3. Show inference direction.
  4. State conclusion and assumptions.

Common wrong answers and the exact correction
  • A true conclusion does not prove its presumed cause.
  • Logical certainty is conditional on reliable premises.

An answer you could write

  • With the rule “raining implies wet street” and an established raining fact, I can deductively infer a wet street.
  • A wet street alone does not prove rain, and a nontriggered rain rule does not prove dryness; other causes and unknown facts remain possible.
