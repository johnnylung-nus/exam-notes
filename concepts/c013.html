Backward chaining（反向推理；由目標追查條件）

Backward chaining starts with a goal and works backwards through rules that could establish it. Their conditions become subgoals.

Recognise（辨識；從資料找出物件或模式） this in a question: IF–THEN, expert rules, known facts（事實；推理中已知的資訊）, justify（給出理由；用情境與證據支持選擇） a decision

Lecture backward reasoning（推理） from a hypothesis（假設；待驗證的解釋）

Worked study example: Using the lecture’s toy throat rules, ask whether the strep-throat conclusion（結論） can be established. Existing facts are temperature 104°F, two-month illness and sore throat.

What this tells us: The engine investigates backwards but finally applies each implication in its valid forward direction. If temperature were unknown, this route would remain unresolved rather than magically producing fever.

How does a goal drive the search for evidence?

  • Backward chaining works from a desired conclusion to the evidence needed to establish it. A rule is useful because its THEN part matches the goal; its IF parts become subgoals. This reverses the order of investigation, not the logical direction of the rule itself.
  • A subgoal can be satisfied by an existing fact or by another rule. If several rules could establish the same goal, failure of one route does not automatically disprove the goal: another route may succeed. If facts are unknown and no supporting rule establishes them, the system should report insufficient evidence.
  • The advantage is focus: asking a few goal-relevant questions can be cheaper than deriving every implication of a large fact set. The danger is circular goals or repeatedly exploring the same unsupported chain. Active-goal tracking and a clear unknown/failure distinction are therefore part of the design.
Implementation steps and their purpose
  1. State the goal to prove.
  2. Find rules with that goal in their THEN part.
  3. Check each IF condition against known facts.
  4. Turn unknown conditions into subgoals or questions.
  5. Continue until the goal succeeds or candidate rule paths fail.

Choose the method from the problem

Useful for checking a specific hypothesis or asking only questions relevant（相關性；是否切合問題） to a target conclusion.

Common wrong answers and the exact correction
  • Do not start by firing every rule from all data.
  • Goal-directed inference（推論；由依據得出結論） does not automatically mean（均值；平均數） probabilistic reasoning.

Implement it and test the outcome

Data, components and application

  • Circular subgoals → track active goals → avoid recursion loops.
  • Failure to prove → distinguish insufficient knowledge from falsity → unknown is not necessarily absent.

How to evaluate it

Check that every required subgoal has evidence and that the final explanation contains the supporting rule chain.

Build the answer in this order

  1. Identify goal.
  2. Select supporting rules.
  3. Expand（展開；產生節點的後續候選） conditions into subgoals.
  4. State proven, disproven or unresolved based on evidence.

An answer you could write

  • To check the toy strep goal, I require sore throat and suspected infection; infection requires long illness and fever, and fever is established by104°F.
  • If temperature is unknown, this proof route is unresolved rather than disproved; I may request that evidence or inspect another supporting rule.
