GA selection operators

GA selection favours fitter candidates for reproduction while retaining some diversity. It decides who contributes genetic material, not how genes are changed.

How does fitness change the chance of becoming a parent?

  • Roulette selection turns suitable fitness values into a probability distribution. A candidate gets an interval proportional to its fitness, and a random draw selects the interval it falls inside. A fitter individual has more chance, but a weaker one can still be chosen and a strong one can be sampled repeatedly.
  • Tournament selection instead samples participants and chooses the best among them; rank selection uses ordering rather than raw score gaps. These choices change selection pressure. Large score disparities under roulette can rapidly duplicate one candidate, while excessive pressure reduces diversity before better combinations have been explored.
  • Selection is not crossover or replacement. It chooses who contributes genetic material. After generating offspring, a separate replacement policy determines which old population members remain. Confusing these steps makes a trace impossible to check and can accidentally discard the best feasible solution.
Implementation steps and their purpose
  1. Evaluate every candidate’s fitness.
  2. Choose a method: roulette, tournament or rank selection.
  3. Roulette: divide suitable nonnegative fitness by total and form cumulative intervals.
  4. Draw/select parents according to the method.
  5. Then apply crossover, mutation and replacement.

Use the result and check it

Data, components and application

  • One candidate dominates → rank/tournament pressure tuning → diversity can collapse early.
  • Raw negative costs → transform or use a compatible selection method → roulette probabilities must be valid.

How to evaluate it

Check probabilities sum to one and random values map to the correct interval. Compare diversity, convergence and final feasible quality.

Build the answer in this order

  1. State fitness direction.
  2. Calculate selection weights.
  3. Show selected parents.
  4. Discuss pressure and diversity.
