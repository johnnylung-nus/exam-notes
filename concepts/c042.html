C042

Sparsity（稀疏性） and cold start（冷啟動）

Sparsity means most user–item pairs lack observations（觀測值）. Cold start means a new user/item has little or no history. They weaken different parts of recommendation（推薦）.

Recognise（辨識；從資料找出物件或模式） this in a question: Ratings, similar users/items, collaborative filtering（協同過濾）, sparse voluntary feedback

What this tells us: The exam’s strong argument is sparse feedback→little shared treatment overlap→unreliable patient similarity. The density illustration must not be presented as a measured fact（事實；推理中已知的資訊） or necessary calculation for the two-mark answer.

Why are missing ratings different from dislikes?

  • Sparsity is not just a large number of blanks: it determines whether the chosen mechanism has enough evidence. User-based（以使用者為基礎） CF（Collaborative Filtering） needs common ratings to compare users; item-based（以項目為基礎） CF needs common users to compare items; MF（Matrix Factorization；矩陣分解） shares latent（潛在） patterns but still needs observations to learn each entity（實體；被描述的對象）’s factors.
  • Cold start is the extreme case of no useful history for a new entity. More latent dimensions or a different similarity formula cannot estimate a brand-new user’s tastes from no evidence. Starter preferences, content attributes（屬性；對象的描述欄位） or an explicit（明確；直接提供的資訊） nonpersonalised fallback（備援處理） supply a different information source.
  • The meaning of one observed entry must be established before computing density. In sample Q3(c), a patient rates a treatment set at a consultation, not necessarily every individual treatment. Converting set feedback into several individual ratings is an assumption that changes the data and may introduce misleading labels（標籤；作比較的正確答案）.
Implementation steps and their purpose
  1. Identify users, items and what one feedback entry actually rates.
  2. Estimate observed density only with justified counts.
  3. Check user/item history and shared overlap.
  4. Use content, onboarding or popularity（熱門程度） fallback where appropriate.
  5. Collect feedback and validate transitions（狀態轉移） to personalised methods.

Prepare the data and validate the representation（表徵；資料的表示方式）

Data, components and application

  • Sparse overlap → content/hybrid（混合式） or validated MF → share useful signals（訊號）, while recognising uncertainty（不確定性）.
  • No history → ask starter preferences/use metadata fallback → MF cannot learn an empty user vector from nothing.

How to evaluate it

Report recommendation coverage（涵蓋率；能服務的使用者或項目範圍） and quality by history length, including new users/items.

Build the answer in this order

  1. Identify missingness.
  2. Explain failure mechanism.
  3. Propose targeted fallback.
  4. State assumptions and tests.

Common wrong answers and the exact correction
  • Blank is unknown, not a zero rating.
  • MF helps share patterns but does not magically solve cold start.

An answer you could write

  • “Only about 40% provide feedback, and few commonly rated treatments make user similarity unreliable; therefore user-based CF is weak here.”
  • “The .4% density is illustrative under a separate-treatment-rating assumption, not an observed case statistic.”

Where this is tested in the sample papers

ISY5001 Q3(c) — User-based CF suitability with sparse voluntary ratings

What the question requires

  1. Identify patients as users and treatments as candidate items.
  2. Use the stated limited voluntary feedback.
  3. Connect sparsity to little common rating history.
  4. Explain why unreliable similarities weaken recommendations.

Proposed answer points

  • A concise negative answer: with 2,000 patients, 300 treatments and only about 40% providing feedback, shared rating history is likely sparse, weakening patient similarity.
  • The recorded feedback concerns a set of treatments per consultation, not necessarily a separate rating for every treatment.

Avoid: Do not claim an exact density from an assumed three treatments per patient. The 0.4% illustration requires unverified individual-treatment ratings.
