C048

Negative sampling（負樣本抽樣）

Negative sampling selects some unobserved user–item pairs as training（訓練） negatives instead of treating the entire missing matrix as labelled dislike.

Recognise（辨識；從資料找出物件或模式） this in a question: Clicks, views, purchases, implicit feedback（隱式回饋）, unobserved items

What this tells us: The training table is much smaller than all user–item pairs. The model learns to discriminate under this sampling scheme, so report the scheme and evaluate on candidates resembling actual serving.

Why must unobserved items be sampled carefully?

  • Negative sampling converts a huge uncertain missing space into a manageable set of training comparisons or binary examples. Observed interactions are positives; some unobserved pairs are sampled as negatives. This is a modelling device, not evidence（證據；已觀察到的資訊） that the user explicitly（明確；直接提供的資訊） disliked each sampled item.
  • The sampling distribution（分布） changes what the model learns. Uniform negatives may be easy obscure items, popularity-weighted negatives may be more realistically exposed, and hard negatives resemble positives and force finer discrimination. Hard negatives also risk selecting attractive items the user simply has not encountered.
  • Training and evaluation（評估） candidate policies must be documented. A recommender（推薦系統） that separates positives from nine obviously irrelevant negatives can look excellent while failing to rank（秩；矩陣獨立資訊的維度數） among thousands of plausible items. Excluding known positives from negative draws prevents direct label contradictions（矛盾；不能同時成立） but cannot eliminate all false negatives in incomplete feedback.
Implementation steps and their purpose
  1. Identify observed positive interactions.
  2. Choose a sampling policy（策略） and negative-to-positive ratio.
  3. Sample unseen candidates, excluding known positives.
  4. Train the classifier/ranker on positives and sampled negatives.
  5. Evaluate with a clearly stated candidate set and sampling policy.

Prepare the data and validate the representation（表徵；資料的表示方式）

Data, components and application

  • False negative → control/review sampling → lack of interaction may mean（均值；平均數） lack of exposure.
  • Biased easy negatives → use realistic candidates and compare policies → inflated evaluation may not reflect serving.

How to evaluate it

Check ranking（排序）/relevance（相關性；是否切合問題） and computational savings. Keep train/evaluation sampling transparent for fair model comparisons.

Build the answer in this order

  1. Explain why missing labels are uncertain.
  2. Specify sampling.
  3. Describe learning target.
  4. Evaluate under realistic candidates.

Choose the representation from the task

Use for scalable implicit-feedback classification（分類；預測類別） or ranking, while remembering that an unobserved item may actually be liked.

Common wrong answers and the exact correction
  • A sampled zero is a modelling assumption, not a confirmed dislike.
  • Hardest negatives are not automatically best.

An answer you could write

  • “I keep observed clicks as positives and sample unobserved items, excluding known positives; sampled zeros remain uncertain negatives.”
  • “I compare sampling policies and evaluate realistic candidate rankings because easy negatives can exaggerate quality.”
