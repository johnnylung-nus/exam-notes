Recommendation（推薦） overfitting（過度擬合） and regularization（正則化）

Overfitting means learning training-specific noise（雜訊；干擾有效訊息的成分） instead of patterns that transfer. In MF（Matrix Factorization；矩陣分解）, too many factors or large weights（權重） can fit sparse（稀疏性） observations（觀測值） too closely.

Regularised MF objective in words

objective = sum of observed rating errors² + λ × factor-weight penalty
λ
regularisation strength
observed
known training ratings only for explicit-rating fitting
  1. Fit user/item vectors to known ratings（已知評分；實際有記錄的評分）.
  2. Penalise excessively large weights.
  3. Tune factor count and penalty on validation data, not final test.

Interpret the result: Select a transferable model, not an exact reproduction of every training rating.

Why can more factors lower training error but hurt recommendations?

  • Overfitting is diagnosed by transfer, not by model size alone. More factors usually allow lower training error, but sparse ratings leave many parameter combinations weakly constrained. The model can fit accidental observations while making unstable predictions for unknown items.
  • Regularisation changes the optimisation objective by penalising unnecessarily large factor weights. This discourages an extreme fit to a few ratings, but a penalty that is too strong can flatten（展平；轉成一維向量） genuine preferences. Factor count, penalty and training duration interact, so compare them through validation rather than prescribing a universal K.
  • Early stopping（提前停止） uses validation behaviour over training time: retain the model before held-out performance worsens even if training loss continues falling. This is distinct from simply stopping when training converges. A final test should not be reused to choose the best epoch（完整訓練輪次） or factor count.
Implementation steps and their purpose
  1. Separate training, validation and test data.
  2. Compare training and validation error as complexity/training increases.
  3. Tune factor count on validation.
  4. Add regularisation to discourage excessively large factor weights.
  5. Stop before validation degrades and evaluate once on final test.

Choose the method from the problem

Use when training error falls but validation worsens, or a proposed recommender（推薦系統） is too complex for available evidence（證據；已觀察到的資訊）.

Common wrong answers and the exact correction
  • Regularisation does not remove the need for a data split.
  • The lecture’s 50–100 factors is a starting heuristic（啟發式；引導搜尋的估計）, not an exam law.

Implement it and test the outcome

Data, components and application

  • Memorisation → fewer factors/stronger penalty → reduce flexibility.
  • Underfitting（擬合不足） → ease excessive penalty or increase capacity → a model can also be too simple.

How to evaluate it

Use held-out metrics and inspect both error curves. Select settings by validation, not a universal factor-count rule.

Build the answer in this order

  1. Identify evidence of overfit.
  2. Name specific control.
  3. Explain why it helps.
  4. Check validation trade-off（取捨；改善一項可能犧牲另一項）.

An answer you could write

  • “K=5 lowers training RMSE to .20 but worsens validation to1.10, so I prefer validated K=3 at .80 here.”
  • “I tune factor count, penalty and stopping on validation; stronger regularisation discourages extreme weights but can underfit if excessive.”
