Tensors and Shapes（張量與形狀）

A tensor is a container of numbers arranged along zero or more axes. In these AI notes, think “a multidimensional array”. Scalar, vector and matrix are all special cases of tensors—not separate alternatives to a tensor.

Start with what you already know

NameExampleNumber of axesShape
Scalar（純量）50()
Vector（向量）[1, 2, 3]1(3,)
Matrix（矩陣）[[1,2], [3,4]]2(2,2)
Higher-order tensor（高階張量）Several matrices stacked along another axis3 or moreFor example (2,2,3)

Small correction to the wording in your screenshot: people often use “tensor” to mean a 3D-or-higher array, but a tensor can also have 0, 1 or 2 axes.

Shape tells you the size of EACH axis

  • Axes/dimensions（軸／維度）: the different directions used to index the numbers.
  • Shape（形狀）: the length along each axis, such as (height, width, channels).
  • Total stored values: multiply the axis sizes.
  • Be careful with “rank”: tensor rank/order can mean the number of axes in programming. Matrix rank means the number of linearly independent rows/columns; that is a different concept.

Image example: Height × Width × Channels

A colour image can use shape (H,W,C), where H is height, W is width, and C is the number of colour channels. RGB (Red, Green, Blue；紅綠藍) gives C=3.

Image[ row, column, channel ]
Small example: shape (2,2,3)
2 rows × 2 columns × 3 colours = 12 values

A batch of B such images can have shape (B,H,W,C): four axes. Some systems use (B,C,H,W); read the stated axis order rather than assuming one universal convention.

Exam use: distinguish the image’s height/width/channel counts from the batch size and from a model’s trainable parameter count.

Knowledge graph example: Entities × Entities × Relations

The RESCAL lecture writes the knowledge-graph tensor as E × E × R.

AxisMeaningTo select one entry
First EHead/source entityChoose entity i.
Second ETail/destination entityChoose entity j.
RRelation typeChoose relation r.
X[i, j, r] → one number describing the stored link
X[:, :, r] → one E×E matrix for relation r

Choose relation r first: take its matrix “slice”.
Choose row i and column j: look up that entity pair.
  • With 3 entities and 2 relation types, the dense tensor shape is (3,3,2): two 3×3 relation slices, containing 18 positions in total.
  • This is a teaching size example; real graphs are often stored sparsely instead of allocating every possible position.
  • Do not mix E with embedding dimension d.
  • E counts entities.
  • RESCAL learns entity vectors of length d and a full d×d matrix for each relation; those learned factors model/score the E×E relation slices.
  • The graph tensor is not itself the same object as the entity-embedding table.

A stored binary entry may mark a recorded edge. An unrecorded/zero entry does not automatically prove a relation is false in the real world. Head→tail direction matters: X[i,j,r] need not equal X[j,i,r].

Quick exam check

  • What is a tensor? A numerical array; scalar/vector/matrix are its 0D/1D/2D cases.
  • What does its shape mean? Name every axis and its size.
  • What does one entry mean? Specify the indices: an image pixel-channel value or a particular head–relation–tail link.
  • How many values? Multiply shape sizes; do not confuse stored inputs with learned parameters.
