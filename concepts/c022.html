KG Queries: One-hop, Multi-hop and Conjunctive

A knowledge graph stores connected facts. To answer a question, identify the starting entity, relationship(s) and unknown answer. The main distinction is one link, a chain of links, or several conditions on the same answer.

Recognize what the question asks

TypeMeaningLecture questionAnswer structure
One-hop（單跳）Follow one relationship from the given entity to the answer.Who is the head coach of the Memphis Grizzlies?Team ─head-coach relation→ ? person
Path / multi-hop（路徑／多跳）The first relation leads to an intermediate entity; another relation leads to the answer.Which person owns the team where Ja Morant is a star player?Ja Morant → team → owner
Conjunctive（合取；條件同時成立）The SAME answer must meet all stated conditions.Who played as a PG for Memphis Hustle AND is a friend of Ja Morant?Players meeting condition 1 ∩ players meeting condition 2

Hop means following one relationship. Conjunctive means AND, not “choose either condition”. A conjunctive query can also contain multi-hop branches; these ideas can occur together.

Multi-hop: connect the steps through the SAME intermediate entity

  1. Start: Ja Morant is given; the owner is unknown.
  2. First relationship: find the team for which he is a star player.
  3. Intermediate result: keep that team, not an unrelated team.
  4. Second relationship: find the person who owns that team.
  5. Return: the owner person, not the intermediate team.
Given: Ja Morant
Find team T such that: StarPlayerOf(Ja Morant, T)
Find person P such that: Owns(P, T)
Return P

T and P are placeholders for the intermediate team and answer person. These predicate names explain the relation roles; use the actual labels/direction in the supplied graph. If ownership is stored as Owner → owns → Team, find the owner connected TO that team; do not silently swap the meaning of owns.

The slide gives the query structure, not a complete answer database. Do not invent an owner’s name.

Conjunctive: intersect the candidate answers

S₁ = players who played PG for Memphis Hustle
S₂ = people who are friends of Ja Morant
Answer = S₁ ∩ S₂

S₁ and S₂ are candidate sets. ∩ means the intersection: keep only candidates in both sets.

These anonymous candidates illustrate the query operation; they are not asserted real basketball facts. A missing graph fact is not proof the relationship is false in the real world.

Common error: returning B or C because each satisfies one branch answers OR (union), not AND (intersection). Both branches must point to the same candidate.

Turn the wording into a graph query

  1. Underline given entities and the requested answer type: person, team, drug, etc.
  2. Circle relation words: coach of, owns, plays for, friend of.
  3. Draw the pattern: single arrow, chained arrows or branches meeting at one answer.
  4. Follow the actual relation directions and keep shared intermediate variables consistent.
  5. Return only the entity/entities satisfying the complete pattern.

Recognition clues: “who is the X of Y?” may be one hop; “the X of the Y that…” often requires a path; “and also / both” signals multiple conditions. Confirm from the graph rather than deciding from words alone.

How the Disease → Protein → Drug → Trial query works

Question: find drugs that target a protein associated with Alzheimer’s disease AND are involved in Phase II clinical trials. The answer must be a drug, not a protein or a trial.

1. Find candidate drugs through the disease/protein path

Alzheimer’s disease
    ─Associated With→ Protein P
                         ─Targeted By→ Drug D
  1. Start at the specified Disease node.
  2. Follow Associated With to find its linked protein(s).
  3. For each protein, follow Targeted By to find drug(s) that target it.
  4. Collect those drugs as the first candidate set.

Targeted By: Protein P is targeted by Drug D. The same fact can be stored in the opposite verbal form, Drug D ─targets→ Protein P. If the graph uses “targets”, look up drugs pointing to P; do not read Protein P as targeting the drug.

2. Keep only candidates with the Phase II trial link

The SAME Drug D
    ─Involved In→ Phase II Clinical Trial

Check this condition for each candidate drug. A different drug’s trial link does not satisfy it. If the question says “currently”, use any supplied current trial/status information rather than an outdated link.

3. Apply AND to the same drug

S₁ = drugs reached by Disease → Protein → Drug
S₂ = drugs linked to a Phase II trial
Answer = S₁ ∩ S₂  (same drug in BOTH sets)

Why both query types? Disease → Protein → Drug is a two-hop path. Requiring the drug also to have the trial relationship adds a conjunctive (AND) condition. They describe different parts of the same query.

What to write in an exam: “Traverse from the disease to associated proteins, then retrieve drugs targeting those proteins. Keep only drugs that also have an Involved In link to a Phase II clinical trial. Join the conditions on the same drug and return those drugs.”

Do not conclude: the drug cures the disease, has regulatory approval, or is suitable treatment. This query retrieves matching graph relationships; trial participation alone does not establish those claims.
