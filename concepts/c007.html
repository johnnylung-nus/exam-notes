Why can a promising-looking move lead to a worse route?

  • The deck calls its route procedure “Hill Climbing (Greedy Best First Search)” and describes selecting by h alone while discarding earlier alternatives, with backtracking at dead ends. Preserve the specific rule when tracing the lecture; do not silently replace it with a textbook global-frontier procedure.
  • The common weakness is that looking only at the remaining estimate ignores the price already paid. A destination can look close from a node reached through a costly detour. Therefore a low h justifies “looks near”, not “this whole route is cheapest”.
  • Conventional local hill climbing compares neighbouring candidates against the current solution and can stop at a local optimum or plateau（平臺區）. Global greedy best-first instead keeps waiting alternatives ordered by h. These retention（留存；讓使用者持續或再次使用）/stopping rules produce different traces, so an exam answer must identify which variant is intended before discussing guarantees or repairs.

Common wrong answers and the exact correction
  • Do not silently rename all greedy search hill climbing.
  • Lower h need not mean（均值；平均數） lower g+h.

Apply the distinction

Data, components and application

  • Local optimum/plateau → restart or annealing（退火） where appropriate → local improvement can stall.
  • Ignoring paid cost → use A* for cheapest paths → a near-looking node may require an expensive journey.

How to evaluate it

Assess route cost, success and search effort. Heuristic-only ranking（排序） does not guarantee minimum total cost.

Build the answer in this order

  1. Name the exact variant.
  2. State candidate-retention rule.
  3. Trace h choices.
  4. Explain why result need not be optimal（最優）.
