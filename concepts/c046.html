RS-18 Implicit feedback（隱式回饋） and preferences

Implicit feedback records actions（動作） such as views, clicks and purchases. It suggests interest but usually does not directly measure dislike or a rating level.

Recognise（辨識；從資料找出物件或模式） this in a question: Clicks, views, purchases, implicit feedback, unobserved items

What this tells us: The appropriate target depends on the product goal. If event weights are used, name them as design choices and validate them rather than pretending the log contains explicit（明確；直接提供的資訊） opinions.

What can a click tell us about preference?

  • Implicit feedback measures behaviour rather than a directly stated rating.
  • A click, purchase or long view can indicate interest, but its interpretation depends on exposure and context.
  • Buying a gift or accidentally clicking does not establish personal liking; no click may mean（均值；平均數） the user never saw the item.
  • Binary preference and interaction strength answer different questions.
  • Mapping every observed event to one records evidence of engagement; weighting a purchase above a view introduces an assumption（假設；答案依賴但題目未明說的條件） about strength.
  • Predicting visit counts estimates activity intensity among observed behaviours, not automatically like-versus-dislike.
  • The absence of explicit negatives changes training（訓練） design.
  • Use an implicit-aware objective, controlled sampled comparisons or a ranking（排序） approach rather than blindly applying the explicit-rating interpretation.
  • Evaluation（評估） should also match the event: predicting clicks may optimise curiosity while missing purchase or long-term satisfaction.
Implementation steps and their purpose
  1. Define the event and what it plausibly indicates.
  2. Choose binary preference or justified event strength.
  3. Aggregate（彙總；把多筆資料合併成統計值） within a suitable time/session（工作階段；一次連續造訪的記錄） window.
  4. Choose an implicit-aware learning/ranking method.
  5. Evaluate against future relevant（相關性；是否切合問題） behaviour and practical outcomes.

Prepare the data and validate the representation（表徵；資料的表示方式）

Data, components and application

  • All unseen treated as dislike → retain uncertainty（不確定性） or use controlled negative sampling（負樣本抽樣） → users may never have seen the item.
  • Subjective event weights → validate alternative weightings → assigning 5 to a share is an assumption.

How to evaluate it

Assess top-list performance and event-definition bias（偏置／偏差；須看語境）. Distinguish predicting strength of positive activity from like-versus-dislike classification（分類；預測類別）.

Build the answer in this order

  1. Identify feedback type.
  2. State preference/confidence（信賴度；前件出現時後件也出現的比例） interpretation.
  3. Choose method.
  4. Explain missingness and evaluate.

Common wrong answers and the exact correction
  • Explicit and implicit ratings are not interchangeable evidence.
  • Observed activity is not necessarily approval.
An answer you could write

An answer you could write

  • “Views and purchases are implicit evidence of engagement, not direct ratings; I state and validate the preference/strength mapping.”
  • “I avoid treating all missing pairs as confirmed dislikes and evaluate the behaviour or user benefit actually required by the scenario.”
