C011

Inductive reasoning（歸納推理）

Induction（歸納；由觀察推廣一般規律） learns a general pattern from particular observations（觀測值）. Unlike deduction（演繹；由前提按規則推出結論）, a pattern supported by examples can still fail on a new case.

Recognise（辨識；從資料找出物件或模式） this in a question: Acquire expert knowledge, elicit rules, discover patterns from records

Illustrative generalisation from an incomplete sample

Worked study example: A toy dataset contains ten staff cars, all Japanese models. A new sample includes staff not observed in the original collection.

All steps shown
ObservationSupported statementUnsupported stronger statement
10 observed staff cars JapaneseAll observed cases have this propertyEvery staff member drives Japanese cars
Only morning arrivals sampledPattern applies to sampled arrivalsAll shifts are represented
New staff car is non-JapaneseOriginal universal claim has counterexample（反例；推翻一般說法的例子）The observation was impossible
Broader representative sample collectedGeneralisation can be reassessedFuture exceptions are now impossible

What this tells us: Induction needs held-out（留出資料；未參與該階段訓練） evidence（證據；已觀察到的資訊） and a clear target population（族群）. The car example demonstrates uncertainty（不確定性） of generalisation; it does not recommend this weak variable（變數） as a meaningful prediction（預測） task.

How do examples become a general rule?

  • Induction compresses a collection of examples into a reusable hypothesis（假設；待驗證的解釋）. It can learn a relationship that was not explicitly（明確；直接提供的資訊） written as a rule, but the conclusion（結論） remains contingent on the observations: a sample may omit important exceptions or represent only a narrow population.
  • The training（訓練） row must correspond to the future decision. In the sample diagnosis（診斷；判斷可能的疾病或故障原因） discussion, current patient features（特徵；描述樣本的屬性） are inputs and the recorded doctor diagnosis is the historical label（標籤；作比較的正確答案）. Including that label in the input would make training deceptively easy while being unusable when the new patient arrives.
  • Learning a pattern and deploying a decision are different operations. The model predicts from its learned relationship; policy（策略） or domain review may still govern what is done with the prediction. This avoids treating a statistical association as a universal rule or assuming a model’s output automatically specifies an action.
Implementation steps and their purpose
  1. Define one training example, input features and desired output.
  2. Collect representative observations.
  3. Fit a hypothesis/model to the training examples.
  4. Tune complexity on validation data.
  5. Evaluate on untouched cases and inspect errors.

Prepare the data and validate the representation（表徵；資料的表示方式）

Data, components and application

  • Biased observations → broaden sampling（取樣） → observed cases may not represent the target population.
  • Memorisation → control complexity and validate → fitting the past is not the same as generalising.

How to evaluate it

Judge performance on unseen representative data, with metrics reflecting the task and costs of mistakes.

Build the answer in this order

  1. Identify examples and target.
  2. Explain learning from instances.
  3. Check sampling and leakage.
  4. Assess generalisation.

Choose the representation from the task

Use when historical labelled examples are available and the goal is to predict unseen outcomes.

Common wrong answers and the exact correction
  • A perfect training fit is not proof of a universal rule.
  • Training labels must not become prediction-time inputs.

An answer you could write

  • The ten Japanese cars support（支持度；同時出現的交易比例） a pattern only in the observed sample; a non-Japanese staff car would refute the universal claim.
  • For historical diagnosis learning, the current findings are inputs and the recorded diagnosis is the target, which is unavailable as an input for the new patient.
