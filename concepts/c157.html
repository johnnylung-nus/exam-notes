K-means（K平均分群）++ initialization（初始化）

K-means++ selects initial centroids that tend to be spread out, then runs ordinary K-means. It reduces the risk that several starting centers occupy the same small region.

How does K-means++ spread out the starting centres?

  • K-means++ changes initialization, not the later objective or centroid（群中心） update. It tries to spread initial seeds so that several centers do not waste their starts in one dense local region.
  • After one random seed, each observation’s chance of becoming the next seed is proportional to its squared distance from its nearest existing seed. This is probabilistic; it does not deterministically choose the farthest point.
  • More spread-out starts often improve convergence（收斂；結果逐步趨於穩定） and solution quality, but distant outliers（離群值） also obtain high sampling weight. Scaling and outlier investigation remain important, and K is still supplied rather than discovered by this initialization.
Implementation steps and their purpose
  1. Prepare feature（特徵；描述樣本的屬性） scales and choose K
  2. Select the first center randomly
  3. Find each point’s squared distance to its nearest chosen center
  4. Sample another center with probability proportional to that squared distance
  5. Repeat until K centers exist
  6. Run normal assignment-and-mean-update iterations

Data, components and application

  • Outlier far from all centers → investigate unusual points → K-means++ may choose it
  • Different starting runs vary → use several starts → retain a strong solution
  • Clusters are elongated → change method if appropriate → initialization cannot repair the model geometry

How to evaluate it

Compare final squared error and stability across starts, along with silhouette（輪廓係數） and meaningful profiles.

When would this calculation be useful?

Use when standard random initialization produces unstable or poor cluster solutions.
