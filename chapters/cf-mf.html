CF and MF: what is the difference?

  • Collaborative filtering (CF): recommend from patterns in users’ interactions with items.
  • Matrix factorization (MF): one model-based way to do CF. Learn a short factor vector for each user and item.
  • User-based CF: use similar users’ feedback. Item-based CF: use feedback on similar items.
  • Explicit feedback: a stated rating. Implicit feedback: observed behaviour; no interaction does not prove dislike.

Reference: Google: collaborative filtering.

How MF predicts

  • R has m users and n items. Choose k factors; P has shape m × k and Q has shape n × k.
  • A predicted rating is the dot product of that user’s and item’s factor vectors.
  • The factors are learned from interactions. Their meanings are not necessarily named categories.
r^ui=puTqi

pᵤ: user vector; qᵢ: item vector; r̂ᵤᵢ: predicted rating.

Loss: what gets fitted?

L=∑(u,i)∈Ω(rui−puTqi)2

Basic explicit-rating objective. Ω contains observed training ratings only.

  • Reduce prediction errors on known training ratings; unknown entries are not zero ratings.
  • Regularization penalizes large factor values to reduce overfitting. Its strength is a validation choice.
  • An observed-rating loss is not automatically suitable for implicit feedback; the treatment of unobserved interactions matters.

Reference: Google: matrix factorization and objectives.

What to examine in a scenario

CheckReasoning to give
TaskPredict a rating, or rank recommendations? Match the loss and evaluation to the task.
Missing feedbackUnknown does not mean disliked. Treat explicit and implicit feedback differently.
Sparse data / cold startFew observations weaken user/item estimates; a completely new user or item has no interaction history. Extra information or an initial fallback is needed.
OverfittingToo many factors can fit noise. Tune factor count and regularization using validation data.
EvaluationFor ratings, assess errors on held-out ratings. For ranking, assess relevant items near the top. Keep future feedback out of training when simulating future recommendations.

MF is not always classical SVD

  • Both use a low-dimensional matrix representation.
  • Classical SVD decomposes a numerical matrix; it does not directly accept unknown entries.
  • Rating MF can optimize only observed entries. Filling unknown ratings with zeros changes the learning problem.
